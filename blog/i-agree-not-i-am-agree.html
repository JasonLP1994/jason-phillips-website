“I agree” or “I am agree”? The simple rule — and better ways to agree.

A very common mistake is saying “I am agree.” The correction is simple, but understanding the pattern helps you avoid several other mistakes with agree.

By Jason Phillips · British English Coach · Published 25 September 2026 · 5 min read
Quick answer:
Say “I agree.” Do not say “I am agree.”

Why “I am agree” is wrong

Agree is a verb. That means it can stand on its own after the subject:

✗ I am agree with you.
✓ I agree with you.

We use am / is / are before adjectives or certain verb forms, but agree is already the main verb here. Compare:

  • I am happy. — happy is an adjective.
  • I am working. — working is part of the present continuous.
  • I agree. — agree is the main verb.

Agree with, agree on and agree to

The next problem is usually the preposition. These three patterns are useful:

PatternUse it forExample
agree witha person, opinion or ideaI agree with Sarah. / I agree with that point.
agree ona topic, plan or decision both sides settleWe agreed on the final deadline.
agree toa proposal, request, condition or actionThey agreed to the new terms. / He agreed to help.

A useful distinction

“I agree with the plan” means you think the plan is good. “We agreed on the plan” means the people involved reached the same decision about it.

More natural ways to agree in professional English

Repeating “I agree” is correct, but in meetings it can sound basic if you use it every time. Try these instead:

  • I completely agree.
  • I agree with you on that.
  • That’s a good point.
  • I see it the same way.
  • I think you’re right about that.
  • That makes sense to me.
  • I’m on board with that. — more informal, but common at work.

What if you only partly agree?

This is where stronger professional English becomes useful. You often need to show agreement without accepting the whole idea:

I agree with the main point, but I think we need more time.

I can see where you’re coming from. My only concern is the cost.

That makes sense, although I’d take a slightly different approach.

These phrases sound more tactful than a direct “I disagree.” They are especially useful in meetings, negotiations and presentations.

Common mistakes to avoid

✗ I am agree.
✓ I agree.

✗ I agree to you.
✓ I agree with you.

✗ We agreed about the deadline.
✓ We agreed on the deadline.

✗ She agreed helping us.
✓ She agreed to help us.

Quick practice

Choose with, on or to:

  1. I agree ___ your assessment of the situation.
  2. We finally agreed ___ a date for the presentation.
  3. The client agreed ___ extend the deadline.
  4. I don’t completely agree ___ that argument.

Answers: 1. with   2. on   3. to   4. with

The takeaway

If you remember only one thing, remember this: agree is a verb, so say “I agree,” not “I am agree.” Then choose the preposition based on what comes next: with a person or idea, on a shared decision, and to a proposal or action.
