“Good” is one of the words I hear far too often in lessons.

There is nothing wrong with good. The problem is using it for everything. More precise vocabulary makes your English clearer, stronger and more natural.

By Jason Phillips · British English Coach · Published 10 October 2026 · 5 min read
The idea:
Don’t replace good with a more complicated word just to sound advanced. Replace it when another word describes your meaning more accurately.

Why “good” gets overused

I hear good constantly in lessons. A good idea. A good result. A good employee. A good opportunity. A good presentation. A good change. A good future.

None of those are necessarily wrong. But when the same adjective appears again and again, it makes otherwise strong English sound less specific.

Advanced vocabulary is not about forcing unusual words into every sentence. It is about having enough choices to say exactly what you mean.

Eight useful alternatives to “good”

BeneficialSomething that produces a useful or helpful result.

Regular feedback is beneficial for language development.

PositiveSomething favourable, encouraging or constructive.

We received positive feedback from the client.

ValuableSomething useful, important or worth having.

The meeting gave us valuable information.

EffectiveSomething that successfully produces the intended result.

It was an effective strategy for increasing sales.

AdvantageousSomething that gives you an advantage or improves your position.

The agreement could be advantageous for both companies.

ImpressiveSomething that creates admiration because of its quality, skill or scale.

Her presentation was genuinely impressive.

PromisingSomething that shows signs of future success.

The early results are promising.

ExceptionalSomething unusually strong, excellent or far above the normal standard.

He delivered an exceptional performance.

They are not all interchangeable

This is the important part. These words are not simply eight decorative replacements for good.

A good strategy could become an effective strategy if it achieves the result you want.

A good opportunity could become a valuable opportunity if it gives you something important.

A good result could become a promising result if it suggests future success.

A good employee might be an exceptional employee if their performance is far above average.

The best synonym depends on why something is good.

Professional English gets stronger when you become specific

This matters especially at work. Compare these:

It was a good meeting.

It was a productive meeting.

It was an effective meeting.

It was a valuable meeting.

Those sentences do not mean exactly the same thing. Productive tells me work was achieved. Effective tells me the meeting achieved its purpose. Valuable tells me it was useful or important.

That extra precision is what makes your English sound more mature and professional.

A simple habit to build

Whenever you notice yourself using good, ask one question:

What exactly is good about it?

If it helps someone, it may be beneficial. If it works, it may be effective. If it is useful, it may be valuable. If the future looks encouraging, it may be promising. If it stands out, it may be impressive or exceptional.

Quick practice

Replace good with the most precise word from this article.

  1. The new training programme had a very good effect on the team.
  2. The candidate gave a very good presentation.
  3. The first test results look good.
  4. The advice was really good for my career.
  5. The new arrangement gives us a good position in the negotiation.

Possible answers: positive / beneficial · impressive · promising · valuable · advantageous

The takeaway

You do not need to ban the word good. Native speakers use it all the time.

But if you notice yourself saying it every few sentences, that is a signal to become more specific. Better vocabulary is not about sounding clever. It is about making your meaning clearer.
