How to Give a Confident Presentation in English: 10 Practical Tips

You do not need perfect English to give an effective presentation. You need a clear message, useful language and realistic practice.

By Jason Phillips · 23 October 2026 · 7 min read

PHILLIPS ENGLISH

English for the moments that matter.

1. Start with one clear message

Before preparing slides, finish this sentence: “After my presentation, I want the audience to understand…” Keep that message visible as you plan.

2. Build a simple structure

Use an introduction, two or three main points, and a conclusion. Tell listeners where you are going: “Today, I’ll focus on three areas.”

3. Practise your opening aloud

The first thirty seconds often feel the hardest. Rehearse your greeting, topic and purpose until you can deliver them naturally without reading.

4. Use signposting phrases

Guide listeners with “Let’s move on to…”, “The key point here is…”, “To put that into perspective…” and “To sum up…”. Choose phrases you can say comfortably.

5. Keep slides simple

Use slides to support your message, not replace it. Avoid reading long paragraphs aloud. One clear chart or example can be more effective than a crowded slide.

6. Slow down and pause

Speaking quickly can make pronunciation and meaning less clear. Pause after important points and give listeners time to process information.

7. Explain technical language

Know your audience. If you need a specialist term, explain it simply: “In other words…” or “What this means in practice is…”. Clarity matters more than impressive vocabulary.

8. Prepare for questions

List five questions your audience might ask. Practise answers with a colleague or tutor. Useful phrases include “That’s a good question”, “Let me clarify…” and “I’ll come back to that point.”

9. Rehearse under realistic conditions

Stand up, use your slides and speak within the time limit. Record yourself once, notice one or two issues and repeat. Practising only in your head is not enough.

10. Finish with a memorable takeaway

Do not end with “That’s all.” Restate your key message and next step: “The main takeaway is…” or “What I’d recommend is…”.

A five-minute practice challenge

Choose a topic you know well. Spend one minute planning, three minutes presenting and one minute answering a surprise question. Listen for clarity, structure and natural phrasing. Then repeat after feedback.
