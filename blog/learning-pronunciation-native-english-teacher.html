Pronunciation · Word stress · Clear speech

You know the word. So why does nobody understand it?

The benefits of clear pronunciation models and feedback you can actually use.

By Jason Phillips · 3 October 2026 · 3 min read

English pronunciation can be a bit rude.

You learn a word, remember what it means and finally get a chance to use it. Then someone looks at you blankly.

Lovely. All that effort and now you’re repeating yourself three times.

Often, the problem is the pronunciation. Working with a native English teacher can help you hear how words sound in everyday conversation and practise using them naturally.

There’s more to it than individual sounds

You might pronounce every sound reasonably well but put the stress on the wrong syllable.

Take “photograph” and “photography”.

We say PHOtograph, but phoTOGraphy. The stress moves, and some of the vowel sounds change too. English apparently felt one version wasn’t enough.

Learning the sound of the whole word, rather than guessing from its spelling, can save you a fair bit of frustration.

Hear what happens in real conversation

People rarely speak one perfectly separated word at a time.

Sounds join together. Some words become weaker. Others receive more emphasis because they carry the important information.

In “I can do it”, for example, “can” often has a weak vowel sound called the schwa, /ə/. When we emphasise it, as in “Yes, I CAN”, the vowel becomes stronger.

Practising these patterns helps you understand spoken English as well as speak it.

A native teacher can model these features naturally and show you how they work. A skilled teacher also explains what to change, rather than simply asking you to copy them louder.

Get feedback on your own pronunciation

Sometimes you can hear the difference but struggle to make it yourself.

That’s where individual feedback helps. We can look at tongue position, lip shape, syllable stress and what happens when you put the word into a sentence.

Then we practise it in something you actually need to say.

Getting “strategic” right on its own is useful. Being able to say “Our strategic priorities for next year” comfortably during your presentation is rather more useful.

You don’t need to lose your accent

Having an accent is normal. Native speakers have them too, and British accents vary enormously.

The aim is to make your English clear and easy to follow. You can keep your identity while improving the sounds, stress and rhythm that affect understanding.

Being a native speaker doesn’t automatically make someone a good pronunciation teacher either. You need someone who can hear the difficulty, explain it clearly and help you practise.

Let’s work on the words that keep catching you out

Bring me the words you avoid, the ones people ask you to repeat or the terminology you need for work.

We’ll work on how they sound, how they fit into sentences and how to use them comfortably in conversation.

No need to apologise to the word “photography” every time you meet it.
