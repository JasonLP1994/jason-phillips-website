Business English guide

Business English vocabulary for meetings.

Useful words, collocations and phrases for contributing clearly, sounding professional and moving a meeting forward.

Knowing individual business words is useful, but meetings depend more on combinations of words you can retrieve quickly. These are the kinds of phrases that help you enter a discussion, clarify a point, challenge an idea politely and finish with clear actions.

Starting and setting the agenda

run through the agenda — quickly review the planned topics.
“Before we start, I’ll quickly run through the agenda.”
the main objective — the most important purpose.
“The main objective today is to agree the launch timeline.”
cover the key points — discuss the most important items.
“We should be able to cover the key points in about 30 minutes.”
come back to something — return to a topic later.
“Let’s come back to that once we’ve looked at the figures.”

Giving your opinion

“From my perspective…”
“The way I see it…”
“My main concern is…”
“I’d be inclined to…”

I’d be inclined to is useful when you want to give a preference without sounding too absolute.

Agreeing professionally

“I’m on the same page.” — informal but common in many workplaces.
“I think that makes sense.”
“I’d agree with that, particularly because…”
“That ties in with what we’ve seen from…”

Disagreeing without sounding confrontational

“I see where you’re coming from, but…”
“I’m not completely convinced that…”
“I wonder whether we’re overlooking…”
“Could there be another way of looking at this?”
Useful principle: acknowledge first, challenge second. It makes disagreement sound more collaborative.

Clarifying and checking understanding

clarify — make something clearer.
“Could you clarify what you mean by ‘phase two’?”
just to make sure I’ve understood
“Just to make sure I’ve understood, you’re suggesting we delay the launch by two weeks?”
in other words
“So, in other words, the issue is capacity rather than cost?”
what does that mean in practice?
Useful when someone gives a vague strategic answer.

Interrupting politely

“Sorry to jump in, but…”
“Can I just add something there?”
“Before we move on, could I clarify one point?”
“If I could come in on that…”

Keeping the meeting focused

get back on track — return to the main topic.
“I think we’re moving slightly off topic, so let’s get back on track.”
park that for now — postpone a topic temporarily.
“That’s important, but perhaps we can park it for now.”
keep this brief
“I’ll keep this brief because we still have two items to cover.”

Talking about decisions

reach a decision — decide after discussion.
come to an agreement — agree collectively.
sign off on something — formally approve it.
give the go-ahead — approve action.

Example: “Once finance signs off on the revised budget, we can give the team the go-ahead.”

Actions and responsibility

action point — a task agreed during a meeting.
take ownership of — accept responsibility for something.
follow up on — take further action after the meeting.
circle back — return with an update; common in corporate English, though it can sound overused.

Example: “I’ll take ownership of the supplier issue and follow up by Friday.”

Deadlines and progress

on track — progressing as planned.
behind schedule — later than planned.
meet the deadline — finish on time.
push the deadline back — move it later.
bring the deadline forward — move it earlier.

Summarising and closing

“Just to summarise where we are…”
“The key takeaway is…”
“So the next steps are…”
“I’ll send a short follow-up with the actions and owners.”

Five collocations worth mastering

  • raise a concern
  • address an issue
  • make a decision
  • meet a deadline
  • take responsibility

These sound much more natural than translating word-for-word from another language.
