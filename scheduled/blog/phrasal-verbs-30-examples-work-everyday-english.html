30 useful phrasal verbs for work and everyday English

Phrasal verbs are everywhere in natural English: we set up meetings, look into problems and catch up with friends. Learn the meanings, then practise using them in situations that matter to you.

By Jason Phillips · British English Coach · 23/11/2026 · Free learning guide

What is a phrasal verb? A phrasal verb combines a verb with a particle, such as up, out or into. The combination often has a meaning that cannot be guessed from the individual words. Put off, for example, can mean to postpone.

Memorising hundreds of phrasal verbs in alphabetical order is rarely the best starting point. Instead, learn a smaller group you can actually use in conversations, emails and meetings. Here are 30, grouped by situation, with examples in natural British English.

10 phrasal verbs for work and meetings

  1. Bring up — introduce a topic. “Could I bring up one concern before we finish?”
  2. Follow up — contact someone again about an earlier conversation. “I'll follow up with the client on Thursday.”
  3. Look into — investigate something. “I'll look into the delay and get back to you.”
  4. Go over — review information carefully. “Let's go over the figures before sending the report.”
  5. Set up — arrange or organise. “Can you set up a call for next Tuesday?”
  6. Put off — postpone. “We've put off the launch until next month.”
  7. Carry out — perform a task, plan or investigation. “The team carried out a customer survey.”
  8. Take on — accept responsibility or new work. “She's taking on a new project in December.”
  9. Work out — find a solution, calculate or understand. “We need to work out why the costs increased.”
  10. Hand over — transfer responsibility or materials. “I'll hand over the project notes before my holiday.”

At work, precision matters. Look into suggests investigation; sort out often suggests solving a problem. If a colleague says, “I'll look into it,” they may not have fixed anything yet.

10 phrasal verbs for conversations and relationships

  1. Get along with — have a good relationship. “I get along with most of my colleagues.”
  2. Come across — discover by chance or give an impression. “I came across a useful article.”
  3. Catch up — talk after time apart or reach the same level. “Let's catch up over coffee.”
  4. Turn down — refuse an offer or request. “He turned down the job because of the travel.”
  5. Deal with — handle a situation. “How do you deal with a difficult customer?”
  6. Figure out — understand or find a solution. “I finally figured out how the system works.”
  7. Point out — draw attention to something. “She pointed out a mistake in the draft.”
  8. Run into — meet unexpectedly or encounter a problem. “We ran into an old friend in town.”
  9. Get back to — reply later. “I'll get back to you after speaking to my manager.”
  10. Keep up with — stay informed or maintain the same pace. “It's hard to keep up with all the updates.”

10 phrasal verbs for tasks, progress and learning

  1. Put together — assemble or prepare. “I put together a short presentation.”
  2. Take over — assume control or responsibility. “Sam will take over while I'm away.”
  3. Check in — make brief contact to see how something is going. “I'll check in with you on Friday.”
  4. Sort out — resolve or organise. “We need to sort out the booking problem.”
  5. Pick up — learn informally or collect. “I've picked up useful expressions from podcasts.”
  6. Fall behind — fail to keep up with a schedule. “I fell behind with my revision last week.”
  7. Go through — examine something step by step. “Let's go through your answers together.”
  8. Move on — progress to the next topic or activity. “Once you're happy with that, we'll move on.”
  9. Cut down on — reduce the amount of something. “I'm trying to cut down on unnecessary meetings.”
  10. Speak up — speak more loudly or express an opinion. “If you disagree, don't be afraid to speak up.”

Phrasal verb grammar: where does the object go?

Some phrasal verbs are separable: with a noun, both “turn down the offer” and “turn the offer down” are possible. But with a pronoun, it must go between the verb and particle: “turn it down”, not “turn down it”.

Other phrasal verbs stay together: “look into the problem” and “look into it” are natural, but “look it into” is not. Learn each phrase with a full example instead of assuming one rule works for all of them.

Quick phrasal verb quiz

Complete each sentence with one expression from this list: bring up, look into, put off, hand over, get back to, cut down on. Change the verb form where necessary.

  1. We need to ______ the customer complaint before replying.
  2. I'll ______ you when I have an answer.
  3. Could you ______ the budget issue at our next meeting?
  4. They ______ the event because the venue was unavailable.
  5. Before leaving, she will ______ her responsibilities to Tom.
  6. Our team is trying to ______ unnecessary emails.
Show suggested answers
  1. look into
  2. get back to
  3. bring up
  4. put off
  5. hand over
  6. cut down on

How to remember phrasal verbs naturally

Choose three expressions that connect to your life today. Write a sentence with each, then say the sentences aloud. Tomorrow, use the same expressions in a short story or a simulated workplace conversation. Spaced practice with meaningful examples is more useful than reading a long list once.
