How to improve IELTS Speaking from 6.5 to 7+.

Moving from 6.5 to 7 is usually less about learning “harder words” and more about controlling your English more consistently.

A candidate around Band 6.5 can often communicate well already. The problem is consistency: answers may become repetitive, grammar may break down in longer sentences, ideas may not be developed enough, or pronunciation may occasionally make the listener work too hard.

The four areas you need to improve together: Fluency & Coherence, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range & Accuracy, and Pronunciation.

1. Stop giving short Part 1 answers

One-word or one-sentence answers limit your chance to show range. A useful structure is:

Answer → Elaborate → Example → Opinion/result

Question: “Do you enjoy cooking?”
Answer: “Yes, quite a lot.”
Elaborate: “I usually cook in the evenings because it helps me switch off after work.”
Example: “For example, I often make simple Asian dishes at home.”
Opinion/result: “I find it much more satisfying than ordering food all the time.”

You do not need all four parts every time. The structure simply helps you avoid answers that stop too early.

2. Extend ideas instead of listing ideas

A common Band 6.5 habit is giving several undeveloped points: “It is cheap, convenient, fast and good for people.” It sounds fluent but not well developed.

Choose one or two points and explain them. Use language such as:

  • “The main reason is that…”
  • “What I mean by that is…”
  • “A good example would be…”
  • “That tends to be especially true for…”

3. Reduce repetition

If you repeatedly use words such as good, bad, important, people, things and very, your vocabulary range can sound limited even when your ideas are strong.

Instead of: “It is very important for people.”
Try: “It plays an important role for young adults.” / “It can have a significant impact on families.”

Do not replace every simple word with a “C2 word”. Natural variation is better than forced vocabulary.

4. Use collocations, not isolated advanced words

  • make a decision
  • play a role
  • raise awareness
  • face pressure
  • have access to
  • take responsibility
  • pose a risk

Correct word combinations make your English sound more natural and controlled.

5. Improve grammar by controlling complex sentences

Band 7 does not require every sentence to be complex. You need a mix of simple and complex structures with reasonably consistent accuracy.

“Although working from home gives people more flexibility, it can also make it harder to separate work from personal life.”

Useful structures to practise include:

  • although / even though
  • whereas / while for contrast
  • if clauses
  • relative clauses
  • because / since / as for reasons
  • which can… / which means… for consequences

6. Do not sacrifice accuracy for speed

Speaking very quickly can make you sound less fluent if it creates false starts, unfinished sentences and constant self-correction. Aim for controlled pace, not maximum speed.

Use natural thinking phrases sparingly:

  • “That’s an interesting question.”
  • “I haven’t really thought about that before, but…”
  • “I’d probably say…”

7. Make Part 2 easy to follow

For the long turn, organise your answer into a simple sequence instead of trying to improvise everything at once.

Simple Part 2 structure:
1. Introduce the person/place/event.
2. Give background.
3. Describe the main details.
4. Explain why it matters to you.
5. Finish with a reflection.

Use sequencing phrases such as “To give you a bit of background…”, “What I remember most is…”, “The reason it stands out is…” and “Looking back…”.

8. In Part 3, answer at a more general level

Part 3 is not only about your personal life. Discuss groups, trends, causes, consequences and comparisons.

Question: “Why do some people prefer living in cities?”
Stronger direction: “One major factor is access to employment. In larger cities, people generally have a wider range of industries and career paths available to them. That can be particularly important for younger workers who are still building their careers.”

9. Improve pronunciation through stress and rhythm

Pronunciation is not about copying a British or American accent. The examiner needs to understand you easily. Focus on:

  • word stress
  • sentence stress
  • clear consonant endings
  • connected speech
  • problem sounds that regularly cause misunderstanding

Read the British English pronunciation guide for practical examples.

10. Record yourself under exam conditions

Recording is one of the fastest ways to notice problems you cannot hear while speaking.

  1. Choose a real IELTS question.
  2. Answer without notes.
  3. Listen once for content and structure.
  4. Listen again for repeated vocabulary and grammar errors.
  5. Repeat the answer once, improving only the biggest problems.

A realistic Band 7 mindset

Do not try to sound perfect. Band 7 performance can still contain mistakes. The aim is to show that you can speak at length, organise ideas, use a reasonably wide range of language and remain easy to understand.
